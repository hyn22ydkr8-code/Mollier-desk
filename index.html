        How the numbers are calculated
        
Superheat is suction temperature minus the saturation temperature at suction pressure. Subcooling is the saturation temperature at discharge pressure minus liquid line temperature. Enthalpy and entropy come from the pressure and temperature you enter, and the point after the expansion valve is found at the same enthalpy as the liquid line, down at suction pressure.

        
COP (cooling) is the suction enthalpy minus the liquid line enthalpy, divided by the discharge enthalpy minus the suction enthalpy. Heating COP is the heat rejected divided by the same work. Isentropic efficiency compares the ideal discharge enthalpy at the same entropy with the real one. Heat lost from the compressor shell and the pipes is not included, and readings at a transcritical gas cooler give no subcooling.

        
Properties come from CoolProp, the open-source library of reference equations of state, so they match published tables closely. Blends are worked out as true mixtures with separate dew and bubble points, and superheat is measured from the dew point and subcooling from the bubble point. The temperature, entropy and volume lines on the chart (and in the PDF) are drawn from CoolProp data built into the app, which is far quicker than working them out live and within about 1 K. Your points and results are calculated live by CoolProp.

        
Properties come from CoolProp data built into the app: saturation values and a correction grid generated with CoolProp, interpolated as you go. This works offline and instantly, and against CoolProp running live, superheat and subcooling agree to within about 0.05 K and COP to within about 1 % (up to about 3 % for CO₂ right at its critical point). Blends keep their real dew and bubble points.
